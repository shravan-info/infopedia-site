About

How Infopedia works

Infopedia is a free, search-first encyclopedia. It takes the interface conventions of Grokipedia — a dark, app-like reading surface, book-shelf discovery, inline citations and a key-facts table on every page — and applies them to a small, fully hand-checked corpus.

How articles are made

Each entry is drafted from primary data, peer-reviewed literature, official records and archival material, then edited for length and clarity. Every empirical claim carries an inline citation, and the reference list at the foot of an article links to the original. Where evidence is genuinely contested — the numbers at Kalinga, the historicity of Indus Valley "yogic" seals, timelines for fault-tolerant quantum computing — the article says so rather than picking a side.

Articles are not directly editable. Readers can propose corrections with a source, which is the same contribution model Grokipedia uses.

Sourcing standard

  • Official statistics and mission records from agencies such as ISRO, IMD, RBI and NPCI.
  • Peer-reviewed papers and standard reference works, cited by author and journal.
  • No claim of a specific figure without a named source, and no invented citations.
  • Uncertainty stated explicitly, with the range of published estimates where they differ.
