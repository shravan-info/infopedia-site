Unified Payments Interface

India's real-time account-to-account payment system, run by NPCI, which turned instant bank transfers into a platform used billions of times a month.

Design

UPI is an interoperable overlay on the Immediate Payment Service (IMPS) infrastructure. It replaced the need to share account numbers and IFSC codes with a Virtual Payment Address — a handle such as name@bank — and exposed a common API so that any licensed app could initiate a transfer to any other participating app.1

Four features drove adoption: instant settlement, a single interface across banks, support for collect requests (a merchant or person can request money), and the separation of the payment identity from the underlying account. The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), a non-profit owned by a consortium of banks and regulated by the Reserve Bank of India, operates the switch.

The economics are unusual. Person-to-merchant UPI and RuPay debit transactions were exempted from merchant discount rate by law from January 2020, so banks and the network recover costs through incentives rather than transaction fees — a deliberate subsidy of digital payments.

Growth

UPI processed a few million transactions a month in 2016. It crossed one billion monthly transactions in October 2019, ten billion in August 2023, and has continued upward, with daily peaks well above 500 million transactions. The BHIM app, bank apps, and third-party apps such as Google Pay, PhonePe and Paytm distribute access; the platform itself remains neutral infrastructure.2

Adoption spread from urban e-commerce to street vendors, autorickshaws and kirana stores, partly because a printed QR code costs nothing and settles instantly. Analysts commonly describe the result as a public digital utility rather than a private payment network.

Feature layers

  • UPI Lite — small-value offline-capable wallet payments for low-connectivity areas.
  • UPI 123Pay — payments for feature phones without internet, via IVR, missed call or app-less flows.
  • Autopay and e-mandates — recurring subscriptions with an additional factor of authentication.
  • Credit lines on UPI — pre-sanctioned credit from banks, introduced in 2023.
  • UPI Reserve Pay / ASBA — earmarking funds for initial public offerings.
  • Cross-border links — bilateral connections with Singapore's PayNow (2023) and several other countries.

Internationalisation

NPCI International has pushed UPI acceptance abroad through bilateral agreements and merchant acceptance in Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Mauritius, the UAE, Singapore and France, among others. The model is usually a link between two national instant-payment systems rather than a direct export of the Indian switch, which keeps settlement in local currencies.

This matters for remittances. India is the world's largest recipient of remittances, and cheaper real-time rails are a direct substitute for correspondent banking fees, particularly for the Gulf corridor.

Criticism and risks

Concentration is a recurring concern: two or three apps handle a large majority of volume, and NPCI has imposed a 30 per cent market-share cap on third-party app providers, repeatedly extended in implementation. Fraud is another — the RBI and NPCI have pushed device binding, transaction limits for new users, and a 'beneficiary cooling period' for first transfers.

Banks argue that zero-MDR merchant payments make the system loss-making at scale, and there are live policy debates about subsidising the network, charging large merchants, and whether a single national switch is a systemic single point of failure. Regulators have also flagged the risk of credit expansion through UPI-linked lending without adequate underwriting.
